Game audio

Sound cues

Game feedback has to land inside the first 80 ms or the player reads it as lag. Every cue here is short, dry and front-loaded — cut to its transient and level-matched against the rest of the set before it went into the build.

Process

From take to build

Sixteen takes went in, five came out. Nothing shipped in the state it arrived.

01Cast wideFour takes for the level-up, three for the reward, three metallic hits — deliberately more than needed, so the choice gets made by ear rather than by necessity.
02Cut the headEvery take is trimmed to its transient. A cue with 60 ms of silence in front feels broken no matter how good the sound underneath is.
03Level-match, stay dryLevels matched across the set so no cue jumps out, and no reverb — the world already has its own, and a wet cue smears the moment.
04Re-check in gameThe final pass happens with the music bed running, because a cue that reads perfectly solo can disappear under the theme.
